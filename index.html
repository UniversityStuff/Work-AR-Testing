BIM models in AR: on a phone today, on glasses tomorrow

Two short demos that place a complete building model on printed fiducial markers (ArUco). They run in Chrome on any Android phone, with no app to install.

Demo 1 · one A4 sheet

The whole model on a sheet of paper

Lay one printed A4 sheet on a table. The building stands on it, scaled to fit the sheet, and you can walk around it.

Models

  • Level crossing, Toivola (Finland): real railway BIM (IFC 4.3) from the Finnish Transport Infrastructure Agency, published in buildingSMART's IFC Rail sample files. About 85 m of track around the crossing, with layers for track, signalling, cables and the technical building.
  • Single-family house and house, road, rail & bridge site: BIM (IFC4) models from buildingSMART International's Simple-Scene sample, CC BY 4.0. You can switch layers (architecture, structure, HVAC, landscaping, infrastructure) on and off.
  • Commercial building: a textured drone photogrammetry scan from Matt1up's drone-building-scans dataset (CC BY 4.0). It is about 22 MB and loads from Hugging Face.
  • Open .glb… loads any glTF/GLB from the phone, e.g. your own drone scan or a converted IFC model.

Setup

  • Print on DIN A4 at 100 % scale, ideally on matte paper: Demo 1 sheet · Demo 2 corner sheets.
  • Demo 2: each sheet names its corner (front left, front right, back right, back left). The arrows point to the side you stand on.
  • Open this page in Chrome on Android over https:// and allow camera access.
  • Good, even light helps. Avoid glare on the paper.

What to point out while people try it

  • Shared spatial understanding. Everyone around the table looks at the same model at true relative scale, from their own side.
  • The phone is the bottleneck. You hold it up at arm's length, you only see the model through a small window, and you can't point at things or take notes while you look.
  • With glasses the same model stays on the table while your hands are free and your eyes look at the model, not at a screen.
  • The tracking and content are the same on glasses. Only the display moves from your hand to your eyes.

Troubleshooting

  • The debug overlay is on by default. Add ?nodebug to hide it.
  • The camera's focal length is estimated from the markers by itself. Hold the phone at an angle with two or more markers in view for a few seconds. The debug lines also show the estimate, the pose error and how much is being drawn.
  • If a red message appears, it explains what went wrong, for example the page was opened over plain http or the drone scan could not be downloaded.
