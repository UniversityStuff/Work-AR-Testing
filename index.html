AR at work: try it on a phone, then picture it on glasses

Two short hands-on demos built on printed fiducial markers (ArUco). They run in Chrome on any Android phone, with no app to install. Each demo measures what holding the phone costs you, so you can make the case for AR glasses with real numbers.

Demo 1 · one A4 sheet

Guided filter change

The sheet on the table becomes a workstation. A virtual machine appears on it and walks you through a four-step maintenance job with 3D arrows and callouts.

Afterwards you see the taps, the tracking losses and how long one hand was busy with the phone.

Demo 2 · four A4 sheets, spread apart

Repair order across stations

Four stations around the room: Storage, Assembly, Test bench and Shipping. The order sends you A → C → B → D, and each station shows live info and your task in AR.

Afterwards you see how long you had no guidance in view and how often you had to aim the phone.

Setup

  • Print the sheets on DIN A4 at 100 % scale. Matte paper works best. Ready-made PDFs: Demo 1 · Demo 2.
  • Demo 1: lay the sheet flat on a table.
  • Demo 2: tape the four sheets on walls, shelves or tables at chest height, a few meters apart. Ideally put them in different directions so people have to walk and turn.
  • Open this page in Chrome on Android over https://. Browsers only allow the camera on secure pages. Allow camera access when asked.
  • Good, even light helps. Avoid glare on the paper.

What to point out while people try it

  • One hand is always gone. Every real step (unscrewing, carrying, plugging in) needs two hands, but one of them holds the phone.
  • No aim, no guidance. The moment the phone drops to your side, the AR disappears. Demo 2 reports how much of the job you did without guidance in view.
  • Taps with gloves. Every "next" is a tap on a screen. In a workshop that means dirty or gloved fingers.
  • Holding still. The calibration step in Demo 2 only runs while the camera stays on the bench. With glasses you just look at it.
  • The tracking and content are the same on glasses. Only the display moves from your hand to your eyes.

Troubleshooting

  • Add ?debug to a demo URL to draw the detected markers.
  • If 3D objects float above or below the paper, set the camera focal length as a fraction of the longer image side, e.g. demo1.html?f=0.85 (the default is 0.75).
  • "Camera needs a secure page" means the page was opened over plain http. See the README for hosting options.
